# Mio's Math Lab: learning brief

Read this before changing the app or planning a weekly update. It says what
"better" means.

## The goal

Mio can solve each kind of problem **on her own**, **explain why it works**, and
**do it on paper at school**. Everything in the app serves that goal:

- Stars, friends, scenes, and animation keep her coming back. They are not the target.
- "Break it down" steps are training wheels. They should fade as she shows she
  doesn't need them, and come back when she struggles.
- Shipping more content is not progress. Progress is skills moving up the ladder
  and holding there.

## Who Mio is

- 4th grade, following Illustrative Mathematics Grade 4. As of October 2026 her
  class is in Unit 2: fraction equivalence and comparison (Lessons 6 to 15).
- Learns best from real-world things she can picture: animals, building and
  testing (Mark Rober style), being outdoors, sewing.
- Reading is hard for her, so everything is read aloud. Explanations are taps,
  numbers, and drawings, never long typing.
- Likes comparing problems best. Likes competing with herself (streaks, best
  score) and checklists.
- Known mix-ups from her worksheets: adds the same number to top and bottom
  (wrote 6/11 for 3/8, 3/6 for 3/4), and gets lost with fractions greater than 1.
  Strongest strategy: making the bottoms match by multiplying.

## The mastery ladder (what drives the app)

Each skill sits on one rung. The app moves her automatically.

| Stage | Scaffold in the app | Moves up when |
|---|---|---|
| Learning | Full step-by-step | 4 of last 5 right, few slips |
| Practicing | Shorter steps: understand + pick a plan, then she solves and explains | 4 of last 5 right and explained |
| Independent | No steps; she explains afterward | 5 of last 6 right on her own, on 2+ days, in 2+ scenes |
| Mastered | Comes back as spaced review (5 days, then 14) | Stays mastered while reviews are right |

Two misses out of three at a stage moves her down one rung. A missed review
moves Mastered back to Independent.

**Challenge rule:** aim for about 80% right overall. When she's at 90% or more
over her last 10 problems, a mission includes one ⚡ challenge problem (hardest
level, no steps, bonus star). Below about 60%, the weekly update adds support:
better nudges, more pictures, easier entry problems.

**The real test is school.** The app proves she can do it in the app. Her
worksheets prove she can do it alone. When a worksheet shows a skill the app
calls Independent or Mastered going wrong, trust the worksheet and add support.

## How the weekly update should decide

In priority order:

1. **Stuck:** a skill at Learning for 2+ weeks, a repeated mix-up, or a
   worksheet mistake. Improve the steps, nudges, and pictures for that exact
   skill, and add practice that targets the mix-up.
2. **Class moved on:** notes or worksheets show a new lesson or unit. Add it as
   new problem types with the same real-world, character-driven, step-by-step
   style. Don't jump ahead of what the notes show.
3. **Coasting:** most skills Independent or Mastered. Add harder variations and
   transfer problems (new scenes, three fractions instead of two, fractions
   greater than 1, word problems with extra information).
4. **Freshness:** a new scene or story set so nothing feels repetitive.

Keep each week small: at most one new problem type, or two new scenes, plus tuning.

## What success looks like over time

- More skills at Independent and Mastered each month, and they stay there.
- "On her own" percentage rising week over week while "right" stays near 80%.
- The adding mix-up appears less often, then stops.
- School worksheets match what the app shows.
